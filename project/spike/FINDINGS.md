# Risk Spike Findings — Newtonian Gravity Stability

The risk identified in A5 was that Newtonian gravitational forces could produce unstable or inaccurate trajectories, especially when a projectile passes close to a massive source. I isolated that risk in a disposable prototype rather than integrating it into the game. The prototype contains one moving body, one fixed gravitational source, and a velocity-Verlet integrator. It uses the softened Newtonian force model, with G = 1, source mass = 800, and softening = 0.05. The body begins on an approximately circular orbit at radius 3 and is simulated for 120 seconds.

I compared time steps of 1/60, 1/120, 1/240, and 1/480 seconds. The maximum relative energy errors were approximately 1.67×10⁻⁵, 1.05×10⁻⁶, 6.60×10⁻⁸, and 4.12×10⁻⁹, respectively. The orbit remained bounded, and reducing the time step substantially reduced the energy error. At 1/240 seconds, the maximum energy error was far below the proposal's 0.3% tolerance.

This result supports using velocity Verlet for ordinary, well-separated gravitational motion. It does not prove that close flybys or the full two-source level are stable; those cases can produce much larger accelerations and need a separate test. I will keep a simple uniform-gravity level as the guaranteed MUST implementation. Newtonian space gravity will remain a SHOULD feature until a close-approach test confirms that the selected time step is adequate. If that test fails, I will use the uniform-gravity fallback rather than risk the required playable game and GitHub Pages deployment.
